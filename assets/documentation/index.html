01 · Purpose

What NeuroQuant is for

NeuroQuant brings scan import, image review, automated segmentation, volumetric measurements, and reporting into one desktop workflow.

◉

Review imaging

Open multiparametric brain MRI volumes and inspect axial, coronal, sagittal, and sequence views. A CT series can be imported separately into its viewer.

✳

Support segmentation

Run the bundled model on the required MRI sequences to produce tumor-region overlays and measurements for clinical review.

▤

Keep work organized

Maintain patient profiles, scan history, segmentation results, reports, AI conversations, and collaboration features through the workstation.

ⓘ
Designed to assist clinical review. The software does not replace a radiologist, neuro-oncologist, neuroradiologist, or the institution’s validated clinical process. Review each image and result before relying on it.
02 · Workflow

A case from start to finish

Use this sequence for each patient. Staff can prepare the case, while a clinician reviews the images and results.

Open the workstation and sign in

Start NeuroQuant, complete the welcome/getting-started pages if shown, and sign in with the workstation account. The model’s local segmentation workflow can run without an internet connection; account recovery and connected features need network and service configuration.

Import the patient’s scan

Select Import Folder for a case directory or a DICOM series. Select Import Files to stage multiple volume files, add or remove entries, and assign their scan types together. The import flow requires FLAIR, T1, T1ce, and T2; CT is optional.

Enter the required patient information

At import, provide the patient’s full name and age. Complete the remaining profile information later from the patient-details management menu when appropriate. Confirm the correct patient and case before continuing.

Review source images before segmentation

Use the slice-viewer mode buttons to switch between anatomical views and MRI sequences. Check orientation, coverage, sequence assignment, artifacts, and whether all images belong to the same study.

Run and inspect segmentation

Select SCAN to run segmentation. When it finishes, inspect overlays in each plane and the 3D view. Review measurements and adjust display opacity or visibility to understand where the contours fall relative to the images.

Save, document, and export

Review the saved database record, verify patient and scan identity, then export a report or data file as needed. Follow institutional rules for where clinical reports may be stored or shared.

03 · Scan preparation

Importing MRI and CT scans

Good organization reduces wrong-sequence assignments. Use one case folder for one patient and one imaging study.

Recommended folder layout

CaseName/ CaseName_FLAIR.nii.gz CaseName_T1.nii.gz CaseName_T1ce.nii.gz CaseName_T2.nii.gz CT/ (optional) CT series files or CT volume

The sequence names are suggestions. Staff can correct every assignment in the import window.

Folder import or file import?

Import Folder scans the selected case folder and its subfolders, finds supported volumes, and groups DICOM images into series. Choose this for a folder of DICOM slices or when MRI and CT are arranged in subfolders.

Import Files opens a staging list. Add multiple volume files, remove accidental selections, confirm the sequence for each file, then import the set together. Keep the assigned files for one case in the same case folder.

Supported volume formats in the importer
NIfTI (.nii, .nii.gz), NRRD (.nrrd), MetaImage (.mha, .mhd), Analyze (.hdr with its paired .img), and DICOM series discovered from a folder.
!
Not every medical file is a scan volume.
DSV is not supported by this importer. A DSV file may require export or conversion by the originating application to a supported image volume or DICOM series. Do not rename the extension to make it appear supported.
!
Before import: do not mix patients or studies in one case folder. Confirm orientation, completeness, correct sequence type, and that any CT series belongs to the same intended case. Keep original source data unchanged and retain a verified backup.
04 · Image review

Review, then segment

The viewer helps navigate and display the imported data. It does not determine whether the images are clinically adequate.

Two slice-viewer modes

Anatomical Views groups Axial, Coronal, Sagittal, and CT. MRI Sequences groups FLAIR, T1, T1ce, and T2. Use the buttons above the viewer to change groups. CT remains a separate panel.

Axial · horizontal planeCoronal · front-facing planeSagittal · side-facing plane

Run segmentation carefully

Segmentation uses four MRI input channels: T1, T1ce, T2, and FLAIR. Ensure each selected volume is the correct sequence, that all four belong to the same examination, and that the acquisition covers the relevant anatomy. CT is not a substitute for a required MRI channel.

Understand the displayed regions

The result includes automated labels such as active tumor core and peritumoral edema, with computed volumes and regional summaries. These are model outputs. They may be incomplete, over-inclusive, or anatomically misplaced, especially with unusual acquisition protocols, artifacts, postoperative change, or atypical lesions.

Use the overlays as a review aid

Toggle layers, adjust opacity, and move through slices to examine the segmentation in context. Verify the visible contours across all planes. A plausible 3D surface or a precise-looking number does not establish diagnostic correctness.

Realistic patient view

After segmentation, select Realistic to build a higher-detail shaded surface from the current patient's MRI and tumor mask. It loads on demand and may take a moment the first time. The surface is an approximation derived from image intensity; its appearance depends on scan quality and preprocessing, so verify it against the slice views.

05 · Results

Measurements and reports

Use measurements to structure review and follow-up, while confirming the underlying image data and segmentation.

◧

Volumetric metrics

Volumes are calculated from segmented voxels and image spacing, displayed in cubic millimeters (mm³). Review core, edema, total tumor, intracranial, and regional values shown by the application.

▤

PDF reports

Export a structured report for review and documentation. Check the patient identifier, scan date, measurements, and intended recipient before saving or distributing it.

⇩

Other exports

The workstation provides data and visualization exports such as CSV, images, animation/GIF, or packaged files in relevant workflows. Choose an approved destination and verify the saved output.

ⓘ
Volumes depend on segmentation quality, voxel spacing, image resampling, and the source acquisition. Compare like-for-like sequences and acquisition settings when monitoring change; do not interpret small numeric differences without reviewing the images.
06 · Records

Patient profiles and database

Enter only the information needed for the authorized clinical workflow and keep the patient identity consistent across records.

At import

The import intake asks for the patient's full name and age so the case can be saved and prepared. The full profile can be completed or updated later through the patient-details menu. Use the supplied date-of-birth guidance and verify age against the date entered.

Local workstation records

The Windows build stores its SQLite database under %LOCALAPPDATA%\NeuroQuant\data\neuroquant.db. Patient records, case history, measurements, and related local data are stored on that computer. Protect the Windows account and device and follow your organization’s backup and retention procedures.

!
Deleting, reinstalling, moving, or replacing a device can affect local records. Before maintenance, use the institution-approved backup procedure and verify that backups can be restored. Do not treat cloud availability as proof that every local file has synchronized.
07 · Connected features

AI assistant and chat room

These features are separate from the local segmentation model and may send information to connected services.

NeuroQuant AI

The AI panel provides informational responses. It is not the segmentation model, does not validate segmentation, and must not be treated as a diagnostic authority. Review every response against the source images, patient context, and reliable clinical references. Avoid including identifiable patient information unless the service and your organization explicitly permit it.

AI chat history is stored locally by signed-in user. If cloud sync is configured, chat messages can also be mirrored to Firestore; attached image files may be uploaded to Supabase Storage.

Collaboration chat

The chat room supports communication among workstation users. Messages and attachments use configured cloud services and require internet access. When an image or file is sent, it is uploaded to the configured Supabase Storage bucket and the associated message is handled through Firestore.

Confirm recipient, destination, access policy, and permission before sharing clinical information. A failed upload may leave a local copy without a cloud copy.

!
Cloud setup is not an institutional privacy review. A public bucket, permissive storage policy, exposed service credential, or shared account may expose data. Administrators must configure authentication, least-privilege access, retention, and monitoring before enabling clinical uploads.
08 · Help

Troubleshooting

Resolve common import and storage issues before repeating a workflow.

The importer cannot find my scan file

Check that the file is a supported NIfTI, NRRD, MetaImage, or Analyze volume. DICOM slices are often extensionless; use Import Folder and select the folder containing the complete series. DSV is not a supported imaging volume in this importer.

The sequence suggestion is wrong

Change the type in the “Use as” column before continuing. Filename recognition is a convenience only. Confirm that exactly one correct volume is assigned to each required type and that T1ce is the contrast-enhanced T1 sequence.

It says a required MRI sequence is missing

Assign FLAIR, T1, T1ce, and T2. CT is optional and cannot fill a missing MRI sequence. If a sequence file is in another folder, use folder import or organize the four case volumes together first.

My DICOM CT is not detected

Keep all slices of one CT series in the selected case folder, preferably in a clearly named CT subfolder. Ensure the folder contains a complete, readable series and that it belongs to this patient’s study.

Cloud image upload was denied

Verify the exact configured Supabase project and bucket, bucket privacy, upload policy, authentication method, and publishable-key configuration. Do not put a Supabase secret/service-role key in the desktop application. If cloud upload fails, check whether the application reports that the local file remains available.

A result or text is hard to see

Switch between light and dark themes in Settings, confirm contrast, and use the application-wide font-size controls. If the issue remains, record the app version, operating system, selected theme, and the steps that reproduce it; do not include patient identifiers in a public issue report.

10 · Policies

Responsible-use policy

Use the workstation only within the authority and procedures granted by your organization.

Authorized use

Use NeuroQuant for permitted imaging review, segmentation support, measurement, reporting, training, or research activities under applicable institutional approvals. Confirm the patient, study, and intended task before import and export.

Protect access

Use individual accounts. Do not bypass access controls, share credentials, expose storage keys, or grant broad anonymous access to clinical buckets. Report suspected loss, disclosure, or account compromise through your institutional incident process.

Manage data carefully

Do not upload or send patient information to unapproved services. Do not use real patient records for demonstrations or public screenshots unless authorized and appropriately de-identified.

Keep the workflow reviewable

Preserve the source data, sequence assignments, patient linkage, and relevant output context. Record manual corrections and clinical interpretations in the approved clinical record, not solely in an AI conversation.

11 · Important limitations

Clinical-use disclaimer

Read this before using model output in any clinical or research workflow.

!
NeuroQuant is a clinical decision-support and image-review tool. It does not provide a diagnosis or treatment recommendation. Automated segmentations, volumetric values, AI-generated text, and reports may be wrong, incomplete, or unsuitable for a particular patient or scan.

Clinician responsibility

A qualified clinician must review the original imaging, confirm that the correct sequences and patient were selected, inspect the segmentation, interpret measurements in clinical context, and make all diagnostic and treatment decisions. Do not rely on a model output alone or delay necessary care while using the software.

Performance is not guaranteed

Results can vary with acquisition protocol, image quality, voxel spacing, preprocessing, lesion characteristics, prior surgery or treatment, and other factors. The software may fail silently or produce plausible-looking but incorrect output. Validate locally before any clinical deployment.

AI responses

Generative AI may produce inaccurate, outdated, biased, or fabricated statements. It may omit important context and is not a substitute for professional judgment, current guidelines, or direct consultation with an appropriate specialist.

Local validation and governance

Before clinical use, the responsible organization should assess intended use, performance, workflow risks, cybersecurity, privacy, human factors, training, and applicable regulatory obligations. This website does not establish regulatory clearance or clinical validation.

12 · About

About NeuroQuant

A focused workstation for brain-tumor imaging review and quantitative support.

Purpose

NeuroQuant was created to bring scan preparation, MRI review, automated brain-tumor segmentation, quantitative measurements, patient-case organization, and clinical reporting into a coherent desktop application. The goal is to make the workflow easier to inspect and discuss, while keeping clinicians responsible for interpretation.

Project and support

This documentation is bundled with the NeuroQuant desktop application. It does not identify a legal manufacturer, hospital, research sponsor, or support organization because those details must be supplied by the project owner. For deployment, policy, or clinical validation questions, contact the NeuroQuant administrator or the organization that provided your build.
